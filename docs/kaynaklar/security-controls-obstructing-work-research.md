## 5. Automated, tireless, or optimizing actors

### 5.1 Optimization can create incentives to avoid control

**FOUND**

### 5.2 An optimizing agent can acquire an incentive to disable an off switch

**FOUND**

> “A traditional agent takes its reward function for granted: we show that such agents have an incentive to disable the off switch”

Locator: p. 220, Abstract.  
Reference: Dylan Hadfield-Menell, Anca Dragan, Pieter Abbeel, and Stuart Russell, “The Off-Switch Game,” *Proceedings of IJCAI-17*, pp. 220–227 (2017). [Paper](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~russell/papers/ijcai17-offswitch.pdf).

### 5.3 Relation to the human literature

**FOUND**

**INFERENCE:** The cited human studies model circumvention through effort, conflicting primary goals, and fatigue. The agent papers model control avoidance as an instrumental result of reward or utility optimization. Tirelessness removes fatigue as an explanation; it does not remove an optimization incentive to bypass a control that reduces expected objective attainment.

Evidence premises:

- Human effort: “when the effort becomes unreasonable, humans make mistakes or cease to comply” — Kirlappos, Parkin, and Sasse (2014), PDF p. 1, §I. [Paper](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/01_4-paper.pdf).
- Conflicting goals: the reported association is quoted in §3.3 — Woltjer (2017), Abstract, Findings. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1108/ICS-02-2016-0017).
- Fatigue: the definition is quoted in §2.6 — Furnell and Thomson (2009), Abstract. [Primary-paper record](https://researchportal.plymouth.ac.uk/en/publications/recognising-and-addressing-security-fatigue/).
- Optimization incentives: the primary evidence is quoted in §§5.1–5.2.
